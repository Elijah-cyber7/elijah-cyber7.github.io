The app

Visitors browse a film catalog, search it, review what they've seen, fill a cart, and check out. Administrators manage everything behind it.

Home and About

A landing page introducing the store, and an About page describing what it offers. Both sit in the navigation on every page.

Story 1

Catalog and search

Films appear as a grid of posters with titles and prices. The search field matches partial titles, case-insensitively, so a fragment is enough.

Stories 4, 5

Film detail and reviews

Full description, price, poster, and the review thread. A quantity selector adds copies to the cart; signed-in visitors get a review form, and can edit or delete their own reviews. Someone else's review shows a Report control instead — reporting hides it from the page.

Stories 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 21

Cart, checkout, and order history

The cart lives in the session, so items survive navigation without an account. It shows each film, quantity, and running total, and empties in one action. Checkout requires signing in and writes a permanent order; every account has a page of past orders with dates and totals.

Stories 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 14

Administration

Films, reviews, review reports, orders, and accounts are all managed through the Django admin, with full create, update, and delete across each.

Stories 17, 18, 19, 20

Responsive across devices

A Bootstrap grid and a collapsing navigation bar carry the layout from desktop down to phone, in any modern browser.

How it got built

Stories as a dependency graph

Twenty-one stories aren't twenty-one independent tasks. Reviews need accounts, orders need a cart, the cart needs a catalog. Build order fell out of the dependencies — auth, then films, then cart, then orders, then reviews, then moderation. Presentation work went last because it couldn't block anything.

Four apps, split by data ownership

home, accounts, movies, and cart. The lines follow who owns which models rather than how many pages each serves, which is why order history lives in accounts even though Order belongs to cart — that page answers a question about a user.

Moving to a real database

The project moved off SQLite onto MariaDB partway through, to work against the same kind of database it would run on in production. Django warned that strict mode was off, meaning the database would silently truncate oversized data instead of rejecting it — STRICT_TRANS_TABLES turns those back into errors. The stale db.sqlite3 left behind in the directory looked exactly like a broken migration until I checked which database the app was actually connected to.

Moderation, and the bug I nearly shipped

Reports are their own model tying a reporter to a review rather than a flag on the review, which keeps an audit trail and stops one account reporting twice. Auditing the stories against the routes at the end — rather than against my memory of writing them — caught the gap: reporting recorded the report but never hid the review.

What I'd change

Prices are integers, so the store can't represent cents. Reviews have no rating, so they can't be sorted or averaged. And the cart only empties wholesale — removing one film means starting over.
